First, is it really the opener?

Before you blame the opener, a few quick checks. Do these only if the door is fully closed and nothing looks broken:

  • Power: is the opener plugged in, and is the outlet or breaker working?
  • Wall control: is the lock or vacation mode on?
  • Remote: try a new battery, and see if the wall button works. That tells you whether it's the remote or the opener.
  • Photo eyes: wipe both lenses and check that each one has a steady light. A blinking or dark light means they're blocked or misaligned.
  • Red emergency release: if it was pulled, the door is disconnected from the opener.

When it's not the opener

  • Motor runs, door doesn't lift? That's very often a broken spring, not a bad opener.
  • Don't touch the springs, cables or brackets. They're under extreme tension.
  • Don't force a stuck door up or down, and keep people clear of it.
  • If the door is hard to lift by hand or looks crooked, call us before using the opener again.

Opener repair questions in Nashville

Why does my door go down and then reverse?
That's usually the safety system. Check that the photo eyes are clean, lined up and showing steady lights. If that doesn't fix it, the opener's close-limit setting or the door may need adjusting.
My opener light is blinking. What does it mean?
Blink patterns differ by brand and often point to a sensor or wiring fault. Your manual lists the codes. Tell us the brand and what you see, and we'll tell you what it likely is.
Can you program new remotes and keypads?
Yes. Tell us your opener brand and model when you call.
Should I repair or replace my opener?
It depends on age, parts, and how often it's broken down. We'll give you an honest recommendation and the price for each option before you decide.
Will my opener work during a power outage?
Most won't without a battery backup. Every opener also has a red emergency release handle for lifting the door by hand when the door is in good working order. If a spring is broken, don't use it, and call us.
